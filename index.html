Edulas,
step by step.

The guide for everyone who uses Edulas at school: administrators, leaders, coordinators, teachers, staff, students and parents. For each feature: what it's for, who can use it, where to find it, and what to click, with real screenshots. No technical knowledge needed.

How to use this manual

1. Pick your role

Choose your role just below. The manual then shows only what you can do.

2. Find the page

Each feature starts with Side menu → Classes: the way to get there. The side menu is on the left on a computer, and along the top on a phone.

3. Follow the steps

Grey boxes like Save marks are the exact words on the button or box to click.

4. Match the pictures

The red numbered circles in each screenshot match the steps. Tap a picture to see it full size.

You don't install anything: Edulas opens in your web browser on a computer, tablet or phone. Your school gives you your sign-in.

Who does what

Pick your role to see only what you can do. Everything stays searchable. A person can hold more than one role in a school (for example Teacher and Academic coordinator), and each role can be for all campuses or one campus.

System administrator: runs the school's Edulas

  • Sets up the school and is its first member.
  • Invites people one by one, or imports a spreadsheet of hundreds.
  • Adds and removes roles, and removes people from the school.
  • Sees the school cover on Today (campuses, people, classes).
  • Plus everything a leader or coordinator can do: classes, timetables, reports, terms, report cards, calendar, standards.
  • Can plan, mark and take registers for any class.

School leadership: sees the whole school

  • Reports: which standards were taught but not learned, class by class.
  • Early warning: students to follow up, with the reason why.
  • Terms and publishing report cards.
  • Creates classes, rosters and weekly timetables.
  • Adds standards, manages the calendar, links families.
  • Posts whole-school announcements and sends whole-school forms.
  • Plans, marks and takes registers for any class.
  • Downloads the school's Excel report.

Academic coordinator: same powers as leadership

  • Everything School leadership can do (classes, timetables, reports, early warning, terms, report cards, standards, calendar, families).
  • Uses the curriculum map to see every class's term and the copy forward tool to reuse units.
  • Acknowledges parent absence reports from Today.
  • Can step into any class to plan, mark or take the register.

Teacher: runs their own classes

  • Plans units and lessons, ticks lessons taught, copies units from another class.
  • Adds resources to lessons and shares them with the class.
  • Creates assignments, quizzes and tests; marks with rubrics, a comment bank and extensions.
  • Builds online quizzes, drafts questions with the AI Quiz Studio, makes AI narrated lectures.
  • Takes registers; sees parent-reported absences on them.
  • Writes report comments, sets weights, prints report cards.
  • Sends forms and announcements to their classes; messages anyone.
  • Other classes are read-only.

School staff: sees, and helps where added

  • Sees every class, plan, gradebook and standards report read-only.
  • Acknowledges absence reports, downloads form answers, messages anyone.
  • If added to a class as a teacher, can do everything a teacher does for that class.

Student: does the work and sees progress

  • Today: classes, a To do list, and "Your week".
  • Hands in assignments (text, a file, or both) and takes online quizzes.
  • Sees marks, feedback, rubric scores and progress by standard once work is returned.
  • Watches published lectures and opens shared lessons.
  • Sees their attendance and report card (when published).
  • Messages the teachers of their classes.

Parent / guardian: follows each child

  • Today: each child's classes and their week, and forms to answer.
  • Reports an absence in advance; teachers see it on the register.
  • Answers permission slips (Yes / No per child) from a phone.
  • Sees each child's marks, feedback, attendance and report card.
  • Messages any staff member.
  • Needs the school to link them to their child first.

Nothing matches that search for this role. Try another word, or pick "Everyone".

PART 1

Getting started

Set up a new school

Edulas home page → Set up your school

Anyone can start a school on Edulas for free. The person who sets it up becomes its first System administrator.

Steps

  1. On the home page, type your School name and Your work email, then click Set up your school. "Free to set up. No card needed."
  2. On Create your account, fill in Full name, Email and Password (at least 10 characters), then Create account. Or use Continue with Google.
  3. Open the confirmation link in your inbox on the same device. It works once.
  4. On Set up your school, check the School name, name your First campus (leave it empty for "Main campus"), and click Create school.
  5. You land on Today. Click Invite people to bring in your colleagues.
  • A school always keeps at least one System administrator.
  • If your school already uses Edulas, ask its administrator to invite you instead.

Sign in, and Continue with Google

Edulas home page → Sign in

Sign in with your email and password, or with your Google account.

Steps

  1. Open Edulas and click Sign in.
  2. Either click Continue with Google and choose your account, or, under "or with email", type your Email and Password and click Sign in.
  3. You land on Today. If you were opening another page, Edulas takes you back to it.
  • Edulas asks Google only for your name, email and Google account ID, never for your Gmail, Drive or contacts.
  • "Confirm your email first." means the confirmation link hasn't been opened yet.
  • A Google account has no Edulas password, so it can't use Change password on Your profile.
  • Sign out from the account menu (your initials, top right) → Sign out.

How people join a school

Side menu → People

People join by invitation only. The System administrator creates an invite link for one person, or imports a whole spreadsheet. Each link works once, only for that email address, for 14 days.

System administratorCreates invites, imports spreadsheets, cancels invites.
Everyone elseReceives a link, opens it, and joins.

Invite one person (administrator)

  1. Go to People and click Invite people.
  2. Fill in Email, choose a Role (Teacher by default) and, if the school has several campuses, a Campus.
  3. Click Create invite link, copy the link, and send it to the person yourself (for example in a message).
  4. Open invites wait under Waiting to join, where you can Cancel one.

Import from a spreadsheet (administrator)

  1. On People, click Import from a spreadsheet. Use Download a template if you want a starting file.
  2. Paste your rows into Spreadsheet, or Open a CSV file.
  3. Click Check. Every row shows Ready or what to fix.
  4. Click Create 3 invitations (the number matches your ready rows).
  5. Save the links now: Download links (CSV) or Print sign-in slips (two per page). They aren't shown again.

Join (the invited person)

  1. Open your link. The page You're invited to Edulas shows the school, your role and your email.
  2. Click Create your account (your email is filled in) or I already have an account. Google works too.
  3. Click Join Cedar Hill School (demo). You're in.

Spreadsheet columns

  • Required: name, email, role.
  • Optional: classes (staff and students) and children (parents: their children's emails).
  • Several values in one cell: separate with |.
  • Role words it understands: admin, principal, coordinator, teacher, staff, student or pupil, parent or guardian.
  • Up to 500 rows at a time. Classes must exist first.
  • When they join, staff are placed in their classes as teachers, students as students, and parents are linked to their children automatically.
Edulas doesn't email invitations yet. The administrator passes the link on (copy, CSV, or printed slips).
PART 2

Every day

Today

Side menu → Today (the first page after you sign in)

Your first page after signing in. It leads with the day: what needs doing now, your classes, and what's coming up. What you see depends on your role.

Leaders & adminsNeeds attention (registers not taken in any class, absence reports to see) and This week at the school: lessons today, attendance this week, work past due, students to watch. Admins also see the school cover.
TeachersNeeds attention (registers not taken, hand-ins to mark), Your classes today with Done / Now / Later, and Students to watch in your classes.
StudentsClasses today (and how you were marked), a To do list (Take the quiz, Hand in), and Your week.
ParentsEach child's classes today, Forms to answer, [Child]'s week, and the Report an absence button.

How to use it

  1. Read the banner first: "No school today: …", "Reduced hours today: …" or "Today: …" come from the school calendar.
  2. Clear Needs attention: each count is a link straight to the work (for example 1 register not taken).
  3. Open a class from Your classes today. A teacher sees "Register not taken" once a lesson has started.
  4. Scroll for Coming up (next 14 days) and the latest Announcements.

Classes

Side menu → Classes

Every class is an exercise book with a coloured cover, its teacher, its students and its weekly timetable. Covers are grouped by grade and show the next lesson.

Leaders & adminsSee every class and create classes.
Teachers & staffSee every class; open the Curriculum map.
StudentsSee their own classes.
ParentsSee their children's classes; each cover names the child.

Create a class (leaders)

  1. Click New class (on Classes or on Today).
  2. Fill in Class name (e.g. "7A Mathematics", unique), Subject, Grade, a Cover colour (Jade, Plum or Ochre) and a Teacher ("Choose later" is fine).
  3. Click Create class. The new class page opens: add the timetable and students there.

A class page

Classes → click a class

The class's home: its cover, and panels for Curriculum, Assessments, Lectures and Attendance, plus the weekly timetable, teachers and students.

Leaders & adminsAdd and remove timetable periods, teachers and students.
The class's teacherJump into planning, marking, the register and lectures.
Students & parentsSee marks and feedback, lectures to watch, and their own attendance rate. A parent sees only their own child in the roster.

Build the timetable and roster (leaders)

  1. Under Weekly timetable, choose Day, Starts, Ends and Room, then Add period. Repeat for each lesson in the week.
  2. Under Teachers, pick from Choose a person and click Add. Any staff role can teach.
  3. Under Students, add each student the same way. (Invite them from People first.)

Attendance registers

Classes → a class → Take the register

One register per timetabled lesson. Everyone starts as Present, so you only change the exceptions.

Teacher / leadersTake and correct registers.
Other staffView.
Students & parentsSee "Your attendance" or "[Child]'s attendance": rate, lates, absences with dates.

Take the register

  1. From Today (1 register not taken) or the class page, click Take the register. It opens on today.
  2. Each lesson has a panel Register · 08:00–08:45 · B12 marked "Not taken yet".
  3. Change anyone who isn't present to Late, Absent or Excused, and add a Note (optional).
  4. Click Save register. You'll see "Register saved · 1 absent" or "· everyone here".
  5. Use ‹ Mon 16 Nov, Thu 19 Nov › and Latest to move between days.
  • If a parent reported an absence, the student starts as Excused with the reason filled into the note.
  • Attended means present or late, out of lessons recorded. Excused absences are left out.
  • You can't take a register for a future lesson, or on a day the class doesn't meet.

Messages

Side menu → Messages

Private conversations inside the school. Only the people in a conversation can see it. The side menu shows how many are unread.

StaffCan write to anyone in the school.
ParentsCan write to any staff member.
StudentsCan write only to the teachers of their own classes.

Send a message

  1. Open Messages. In New message, pick a person in To (listed as "Name · Role").
  2. Write a Subject and your Message, then click Send.
  3. Replies appear in the conversation (it refreshes every 15 seconds). Type in Write a reply and Send.
  4. Staff can also start from a person's page with Write a message.

Announcements

Today → All announcements

Notices for the whole school, a group, or one class. The newest three also appear on Today. Open the full list from Today (All announcements) or from Messages.

Leaders & adminsPost to the whole school (Everyone, Staff, Students and parents, Parents, Students) or any class. Remove any.
TeachersPost to their own classes; remove their own.
EveryoneReads the announcements meant for them.

Post one

  1. Open Announcements. Under For, choose an audience from The whole school or One class.
  2. Write a Title and the Announcement, then click Post.
PART 3

Plan

Curriculum planning

Classes → a class → Open the curriculum

Plan each class's term as units and lessons, tag lessons with standards, and tick lessons off as they're taught. The coverage panel shows which standards are taught, planned, or not planned yet.

Teacher / leadersPlan, edit, tick taught, copy units.
Other staffView.
Students & parentsSee the plan; open lessons once they're shared.

Steps

  1. Open a class → Start planning (or Open the curriculum).
  2. Click New unit: Unit title, Starts, Ends, Summary (optional) → Add unit.
  3. In the unit, open Add a lesson: Lesson title, Date (defaults to the next timetabled lesson), Objective (optional), and tick the Standards this lesson teaches → Add lesson.
  4. After teaching, click Mark taught: a red tick appears in the margin. Undo clears it.
  5. Watch Standards coverage: "2 of 6 taught" with a chip per standard: Taught Planned Not planned.
Delete unit removes the unit and all its lessons. It asks first and can't be undone.

Lesson pages & resources

A class's curriculum → click a lesson's title

Each lesson has its own page with its objective, standards and resources: links, notes and files. Share it, and the class's students and their parents can open it.

  1. Click a lesson's title in the plan.
  2. Under Add to this lesson, choose a tab:
    • Link: Title + Web address → Add link
    • Note: Title + Note (up to 4,000 characters) → Add note
    • File: worksheets, slides, images or audio, up to 20 MB → Upload file
  3. Click Share with 7A Mathematics. Staff see "Shared 27 Oct: the class's students and their parents can open this page." Use Stop sharing to hide it again.

Copy units forward

A class's curriculum → Copy units from another class

Reuse units and lessons from another class (or last year's class). Copied lessons are re-dated to this class's timetable from the day you choose, skipping school holidays.

  1. On the class's curriculum page, find Copy units from another class.
  2. Choose From (classes with the same subject are listed first).
  3. Under Units, untick any you don't want (all are ticked).
  4. Set First lesson on and click Copy units.
  5. You'll see "Copied 2 units and 8 lessons, planned 15 Nov to 10 Dec."
  • Without a timetable, lessons keep their original spacing.
  • Up to 40 units per copy.

Curriculum map

Classes → Curriculum map

Every class's term on one timeline. Each unit is a bar; its shaded part is what's been taught. Holidays are hatched columns and a line marks today. Staff only.

  1. Open Classes → Curriculum map (also on a class's curriculum page).
  2. Filter with the subject chips: All subjects, Mathematics, Science…
  3. Spot classes falling behind (short shading left of the today line) and units with no dates under Not on the map yet.

Standards

Side menu → Standards

The school's list of learning standards (e.g. MA.7.RP.1). Lessons and assessments are tagged with them, which is what makes "taught but not learned" possible.

Leaders & adminsAdd and remove standards.
Teachers & staffView, and tag them on lessons and work.

Add standards (leaders)

  1. Click Add standards. Choose Subject and Grade.
  2. Paste into Standards, one per line: the code, a space, then the description. Paste straight from your curriculum document.
  3. Click Add standards: "12 standards added; 2 skipped because the code already exists."
  • Up to 300 per paste. Codes are unique; existing codes are skipped, never overwritten.
  • Removing a standard removes its tag from every lesson (Edulas warns you how many).

School calendar

Side menu → Calendar

Holidays, events, exams, reduced-hours days and term dates for everyone. Holidays change how Edulas behaves: Today says "No school today", registers aren't due, and copied lessons skip them.

Leaders & adminsAdd and remove days.
EveryoneView the month and Coming up.

Add to the calendar (leaders)

  1. Under Add to the calendar, type a Name (e.g. "Eid al-Fitr holiday").
  2. Choose the Kind of day: Holiday, no school Event Exams Reduced hours.
  3. Set First day and Last day (up to a year), add a Note (optional) such as "School finishes at 12:30.", then click Add to the calendar.
PART 4

Teach & assess

Lecture Studio: AI narrated lectures

Classes → a class → Lectures

Upload slides and the Lecture Studio writes a narration for each slide, records a narrated video, writes a transcript, and drafts a quiz. You review the narration before anything is recorded, and publish when it's right.

Teacher / leadersMake, edit, publish and delete lectures.
Other staffView everything, no editing.
Students & parentsWatch published lectures with the transcript.

Make a lecture

  1. On the class page, click Make a lecture → New lecture.
  2. Give a Title, upload Slides (a PDF, a PowerPoint file, or typed notes, up to 50 MB), choose the Narration language (same as the slides, English or Arabic), and adjust the Instructions for the narrator.
  3. Click Make the narration. You can leave the page; it updates itself.
  4. When it says Narration ready for review, read each Slide box, edit, and Save narration.
  5. Click Make the video. When it's Ready, watch it with its transcript.
  6. Click Publish to 7A Mathematics. Optional: Make an online quiz turns the AI quiz into a real online quiz.
The narration and quiz are written by AI: check them before publishing. Quiz answers are shown to staff only. The Studio makes one lecture at a time, so yours may wait in a queue.

Question bank & AI Quiz Studio

Side menu → Questions

A shared bank of multiple-choice questions tagged with standards. Write questions yourself, or paste a lesson and let the AI Quiz Studio draft them. Drafts usually appear within a minute, and nothing joins the bank until you approve it.

Write a question

  1. Under New question, type the Question and up to six Answers; pick the right one with its radio button.
  2. Add Why it's right (shown after marking) and tick Standards it checks → Add to the bank.

Draft questions with AI

  1. Fill in What are they for? (e.g. "Unit rates check").
  2. Paste notes or a text into Material (up to 20,000 characters), or leave it empty to write from the standards you tick.
  3. Choose How many (3, 5, 8, 10 or 15), the Language, optional Instructions, and Standards to tag them with.
  4. Click Draft questions. A panel AI drafts · Unit rates check shows "Writing…", then "5 to review".
  5. Untick any you don't want and click Add the ticked questions. They're labelled "AI-written, approved by [you]".
  • Search with Search and filter by Standard.
  • You can remove your own questions; leaders can remove any. Quizzes that use a removed question lose it.

Assessments & marking

Classes → a class → Open assessments

Set assignments, quizzes and tests; mark them on one sheet; then return them. Until you return the work, only staff can see the marks.

Teacher / leadersCreate, mark, return, delete.
Other staffRead-only marking view.
Students & parents"Your work" / "[Child]'s work": score, feedback, rubric scores and Progress by standard, after return.

Create and mark

  1. Class page → Open assessments → New assessment.
  2. Fill in Title, Type (Assignment, Quiz, Test), Marked out of, Due, tick Students answer it online for an online quiz, and tick Standards it assesses.
  3. Click Create and start marking.
  4. For each student, type the mark (or tick Missing), set Extension until if needed, and write feedback (click a saved comment to insert it).
  5. Click Save marks, then Return to students when you're ready for students and parents to see it.
  • A mark can't be above the maximum, and you can't give a mark and tick Missing.
  • Clearing a row (no mark, not missing, no feedback) removes that student's mark.
  • Take back from students hides returned marks again.
Delete removes the work and all its marks. It asks first and can't be undone.

Rubrics, comment bank, weights & extensions

Open a piece of work you're marking

Mark against named criteria and the total adds itself up. Keep the comments you use often one click away. Give a student more time without changing everyone's due date.

Add a rubric

  1. On the marking page, open Add a rubric.
  2. For each criterion, type a name (e.g. "Uses the right units"), optionally What earns full points, and its points. Use Add a criterion for more.
  3. Click Save rubric: "Rubric saved. The work is now out of 20."
  4. Each student now gets one box per criterion and a running Total.

Comment bank

  1. In Your saved comments, type a comment (up to 300 characters) and click Save.
  2. Under any student's feedback box, click the comment to add it. Your comments follow you to every marking sheet.

Extension

  1. On the student's row, set Extension until. Their due date shows as "due 12 Nov (extended)" and a hand-in before then isn't late.
  • Up to 20 criteria; not used for online quizzes.
  • Score every criterion or none.
  • Students see the rubric under How it's marked when handing in, and their scores per criterion once it's returned.
  • Saving a rubric with no criteria removes it.

Online quizzes

Classes → a class → Open assessments → an online quiz

Multiple-choice quizzes from the question bank, answered in Edulas and marked automatically.

Teacher

  1. Create an assessment with Students answer it online ticked.
  2. In Add from the question bank, click Add next to each question (questions with the quiz's standards come first).
  3. Click Open to students. Later, Close the quiz (and Reopen if needed).
  4. Check Handed in ("4 of 5", See answers) and Return to students so they see their score and answers.

Student

  1. On Today's To do, click Take the quiz.
  2. Choose one answer per question and click Hand in. You can hand in once.
  3. After it's returned, see your score and each answer marked right or not right, with the explanation.
Once any student has answered, the quiz's questions are locked.

Handing in work

Today → To do → Hand in

Students hand in assignments: write in the box, attach a file, or both. Teachers see it on the marking sheet; parents can view it.

  1. Click Hand in from Today's To do or the class's assessments.
  2. Read How it's marked if there's a rubric.
  3. Type in Your work and/or attach a File (a document, a photo of your work, slides or audio, up to 20 MB).
  4. Click Hand in: "Handed in."
  5. Until the teacher returns it you can Update hand-in, Replace the file, or Take it back.
  • After the due date (or your extension), a hand-in is marked late.
  • Once the teacher returns the work, it can't be changed.
  • Quizzes and tests on paper don't have a hand-in.
  • Parents see See hand-in (read-only).

Standards report: taught ≠ learned

Classes → a class → Standards report

For one class, the answer first: which standards were taught but not learned yet. Then each standard with its lessons, assessments, average and how many students are Secure, Developing or Not yet.

  1. Class page → Assessments → Standards report.
  2. Read the headline: "1 standard was taught but not learned yet."
  3. Under By standard, click Show each student to see who needs help.
  • Taught, not learned: taught and assessed, and the class average is under 60%, or half the class is Not yet.
  • Taught, not assessed: no evidence of learning yet.
  • A student's level is the average of their marks on work that assesses the standard. Missing work is listed but left out of the average.
PART 5

Report

Gradebook, terms, weights & report comments

Classes → a class → Gradebook

One grid per class and term: every returned piece of work, each student's term average and level. Write the term's report comments underneath.

Set up terms (leaders)

  1. Go to Reports → Terms and report cards. Type the Term ("Term 1"), First day, Last day → Add term. Terms appear on the calendar too.

Use the gradebook (teachers)

  1. Class page → Gradebook. Switch term with the term chips.
  2. Read Marks: one column per piece of work, then Term average with its level.
  3. Open Change the weights to weight Assignments, Quizzes and Tests (e.g. 20 / 30 / 50) → Save weights. All 0 = every piece counts equally.
  4. Under Report comments · Term 1, write "What went well this term, and what to work on next" for each student → Save comments.
  5. Use Preview report card, Download Excel or Print report cards.
  • Work belongs to the term its due date falls in. Only returned work counts; missing work is left out of averages.
  • No terms yet? The gradebook shows all work, with a link to set terms up under Reports.

Report cards

Side menu → Report card (students and parents) · Gradebook (staff)

A report card per student per term: attendance, each class's average and level, the teacher's comment, and progress by standard. Students and parents see it once leaders publish the term.

LeadersPublish a term: Reports → Terms and report cards → Publish report cards (or Unpublish).
Teachers & staffPreview any student, print a whole class: one per page, or Save as PDF.
StudentsOpen Report card in the side menu.
ParentsOpen Report card and switch child with the name chips.

Print a class (teachers)

  1. Gradebook → Print report cards. The page says "24 students, one per page."
  2. Press print, or choose Save as PDF in the print window.

See yours (students and parents)

  1. Click Report card. Print it with Print or save as PDF.

School reports & early warning

Side menu → Reports

For leaders: the whole school in one page. Which standards were taught but not learned, class by class, and which students to follow up and why.

  1. Open Reports (System administrator, School leadership and Academic coordinator only).
  2. Read the answer first: "1 standard is taught but not learned yet, in 1 class", with a link to the class.
  3. Scan By class: lessons and standards taught, average learning, attendance.
  4. Work through Early warning: each student has reason chips and links to their report card.
  5. Click Download Term 1 as Excel for the whole school.

Early warning flags a student when

  • attendance is under 90% across their classes, or
  • their average is under 60% in a class, or
  • they have 2 or more standards "Not yet" in a class, or
  • they have 2 or more pieces of missing work this term.

Excel & print exports

Gradebook, Reports and Forms pages

Take your information out of Edulas as Excel spreadsheets or printed pages.

Class gradebookDownload Excel: sheets Marks, Attendance (lessons recorded, absent, %) and Report comments. A simpler file with marks only is also available.
Whole schoolDownload Term 1 as Excel on Reports: sheets Classes, Students (average, level, missing, attendance, lessons absent) and Students to watch (with why).
Report cardsPrint report cards for a whole class, one per page, or Print or save as PDF for one student.
Form answersDownload answers (a spreadsheet file) on a form's page.
InvitationsDownload links (CSV) and Print sign-in slips after an import.
PART 6

Families

Absences reported by parents

Side menu → Absences

Parents tell the school in advance that their child will be away. Teachers see it on the register, already marked Excused.

ParentsReport; withdraw before it's acknowledged.
StaffSee every report and Acknowledge. Leaders see "absence reports to see" on Today.
StudentsSee reports made for them.

Report an absence (parents)

  1. On Today, click Report an absence.
  2. Choose the Child (if you have more than one), First day away and Last day away (up to 60 days).
  3. Pick a Reason: Illness, Medical or dental appointment, Family reasons, Religious observance, Travel or Other. Add a Note for the school if useful.
  4. Click Report the absence: "Sent to the school. Their teachers will see it on the register."
  • A report can't be edited once sent: withdraw it and report again.

Forms & consent (permission slips)

Side menu → Forms

Send a permission slip to a class or the whole school. Parents answer Yes or No for each child, from any phone, with an optional note. You see who said yes, who said no, and who hasn't answered.

LeadersSend to any class or every student in the school.
TeachersSend to their own classes.
StaffSee forms; Download answers.
ParentsAnswer for each child; change it while the form is open.
StudentsSee forms and their parents' answers.

Send a form (staff)

  1. Open Forms → New form. Type a Title ("Museum trip on Thursday") and What parents are agreeing to (where and when, the cost, what to bring, who to contact).
  2. Choose For (a class or the whole school) and an optional Reply by date.
  3. Click Send to families. Follow the count: "18 of 24 answered · 17 yes · 1 no · 6 waiting". Close answers when you're done.

Answer a form (parents)

  1. On Today, under Forms to answer, click Answer.

Words & rules

Secure

80% or more on the work that assesses a standard.

Developing

60% to 79%.

Not yet

Under 60%. Not a fail: not there yet.

Taught, not learned

Taught and assessed, and the class average is under 60%, or half the class is Not yet.

Taught, not assessed

Lessons ticked taught, but no marked work on it yet.

Returned

Students and parents see marks, feedback and rubric scores only after the teacher returns the work.

Term average

Average of returned work due in the term; missing work left out. Weighted by Assignments / Quizzes / Tests if set.

Attended

Present or late, out of lessons recorded. Excused absences are left out.

Early warning

Attendance under 90%, a class average under 60%, 2+ standards Not yet in a class, or 2+ missing pieces this term.

Shared

A lesson page students and their parents can open.

Published

A lecture the class can watch, or a term whose report cards students and parents can see.

Extension

A later due date for one student; their hand-in isn't late before it.

Limits & things you can't undo

Sizes and counts
  • Lesson files and hand-ins: up to 20 MB.
  • Lecture slides: up to 50 MB (a PDF, a PowerPoint file, or typed notes). Save very old PowerPoint files in the newer format first.
  • Importing people: up to 500 rows at a time. Adding standards: up to 300 at a time.
  • Invite links: one use, one email address, 14 days.
  • Absence reports: up to 60 days. Calendar items: up to a year.
  • Rubrics: up to 20 criteria. Questions: 2 to 6 answers.
These ask first, then can't be undone
  • Deleting a unit and its lessons.
  • Deleting a piece of work and all its marks.
  • Deleting a lecture, its video and its quiz.
  • Deleting a form and every answer.
  • Removing a term (its report comments go too).
  • Removing someone from the school.
  • Online quiz questions lock once a student answers; a student hands in a quiz once.
Not available yet: invitation emails sent by Edulas, a "Forgot password" link, and adding campuses after setup. For now the administrator shares invite links, and campuses are set when the school is created.
