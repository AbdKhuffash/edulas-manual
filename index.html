The manual for everyone who uses Edulas: system administrators, school leaders, coordinators, teachers, school staff, students and parents. Every feature, who can use it, and the exact clicks.

Who does what

Pick your role to see only what you can do. Everything stays searchable. A person can hold more than one role in a school (for example Teacher and Academic coordinator), and each role can be for all campuses or one campus.

System administrator: runs the school's Edulas

  • Sets up the school and is its first member.
  • Invites people one by one, or imports a spreadsheet of hundreds.
  • Adds and removes roles, and removes people from the school.
  • Sees the school cover on Today (campuses, people, classes).
  • Plus everything a leader or coordinator can do: classes, timetables, reports, terms, report cards, calendar, standards.
  • Can plan, mark and take registers for any class.

School leadership: sees the whole school

  • Reports: which standards were taught but not learned, class by class.
  • Early warning: students to follow up, with the reason why.
  • Terms and publishing report cards.
  • Creates classes, rosters and weekly timetables.
  • Adds standards, manages the calendar, links families.
  • Posts whole-school announcements and sends whole-school forms.
  • Plans, marks and takes registers for any class.
  • Downloads the school's Excel report.

Academic coordinator: same powers as leadership

  • Everything School leadership can do (classes, timetables, reports, early warning, terms, report cards, standards, calendar, families).
  • Uses the curriculum map to see every class's term and the copy forward tool to reuse units.
  • Acknowledges parent absence reports from Today.
  • Can step into any class to plan, mark or take the register.

Teacher: runs their own classes

  • Plans units and lessons, ticks lessons taught, copies units from another class.
  • Adds resources to lessons and shares them with the class.
  • Creates assignments, quizzes and tests; marks with rubrics, a comment bank and extensions.
  • Builds online quizzes, drafts questions with the AI Quiz Studio, makes AI narrated lectures.
  • Takes registers; sees parent-reported absences on them.
  • Writes report comments, sets weights, prints report cards.
  • Sends forms and announcements to their classes; messages anyone.
  • Other classes are read-only.

School staff: sees, and helps where added

  • Sees every class, plan, gradebook and standards report read-only.
  • Acknowledges absence reports, downloads form answers, messages anyone.
  • If added to a class as a teacher, can do everything a teacher does for that class.

Student: does the work and sees progress

  • Today: classes, a To do list, and "Your week".
  • Hands in assignments (text, a file, or both) and takes online quizzes.
  • Sees marks, feedback, rubric scores and progress by standard once work is returned.
  • Watches published lectures and opens shared lessons.
  • Sees their attendance and report card (when published).
  • Messages the teachers of their classes.

Parent / guardian: follows each child

  • Today: each child's classes and their week, and forms to answer.
  • Reports an absence in advance; teachers see it on the register.
  • Answers permission slips (Yes / No per child) from a phone.
  • Sees each child's marks, feedback, attendance and report card.
  • Messages any staff member.
  • Needs the school to link them to their child first.

Nothing matches that search for this role. Try another word, or pick "Everyone".

PART 1

Getting started

Set up a new school

/ → /signup → /onboarding

Anyone can start a school on Edulas for free. The person who sets it up becomes its first System administrator.

Steps

  1. On the home page, type your School name and Your work email, then click Set up your school. "Free to set up. No card needed."
  2. On Create your account, fill in Full name, Email and Password (at least 10 characters), then Create account. Or use Continue with Google.
  3. Open the confirmation link in your inbox on the same device. It works once.
  4. On Set up your school, check the School name, name your First campus (leave it empty for "Main campus"), and click Create school.
  5. You land on Today. Click Invite people to bring in your colleagues.

Sign in, and Continue with Google

/login

Sign in with your email and password, or with your Google account.

Steps

  1. Open Edulas and click Sign in.
  2. Either click Continue with Google and choose your account, or, under "or with email", type your Email and Password and click Sign in.
  3. You land on Today. If you were opening another page, Edulas takes you back to it.
  • Edulas asks Google only for your name, email and Google account ID, never for your Gmail, Drive or contacts.
  • "Confirm your email first." means the confirmation link hasn't been opened yet.
  • A Google account has no Edulas password, so it can't use Change password on Your profile.
  • Sign out from the account menu (your initials, top right) → Sign out.

How people join a school

/people · /people/import · /invite/…

People join by invitation only. The System administrator creates an invite link for one person, or imports a whole spreadsheet. Each link works once, only for that email address, for 14 days.

System administratorCreates invites, imports spreadsheets, cancels invites.
Everyone elseReceives a link, opens it, and joins.

Invite one person (administrator)

  1. Go to People and click Invite people.
  2. Fill in Email, choose a Role (Teacher by default) and, if the school has several campuses, a Campus.
  3. Click Create invite link, copy the link, and send it to the person yourself (for example in a message).
  4. Open invites wait under Waiting to join, where you can Cancel one.

Import from a spreadsheet (administrator)

  1. On People, click Import from a spreadsheet. Use Download a template if you want a starting file.
  2. Paste rows into Spreadsheet, or Open a CSV file.
  3. Click Check. Every row shows Ready or what to fix.
  4. Click Create 3 invitations (the number matches your ready rows).
  5. Save the links now: Download links (CSV) or Print sign-in slips (two per page). They aren't shown again.

Join (the invited person)

  1. Open your link. The page You're invited to Edulas shows the school, your role and your email.
  2. Click Create your account (your email is filled in) or I already have an account. Google works too.
  3. Click Join Cedar Hill School (demo). You're in.

  • Required: name, email, role.
  • Optional: classes (staff and students) and children (parents: their children's emails).

Today

/dashboard

Your first page after signing in. It leads with the day: what needs doing now, your classes, and what's coming up. What you see depends on your role.

Leaders & adminsNeeds attention (registers not taken in any class, absence reports to see) and This week at the school: lessons today, attendance this week, work past due, students to watch. Admins also see the school cover.

How to use it

  1. Read the banner first: "No school today: …", "Reduced hours today: …" or "Today: …" come from the school calendar.
  2. Clear Needs attention: each count is a link straight to the work (for example 1 register not taken).
  3. Open a class from Your classes today. A teacher sees "Register not taken" once a lesson has started.
  4. Scroll for Coming up (next 14 days) and the latest Announcements.

Plan

Curriculum planning

/classes/{class}/plan

Plan each class's term as units and lessons, tag lessons with standards, and tick lessons off as they're taught. The coverage panel shows which standards are taught, planned, or not planned yet.

Teacher / leadersPlan, edit, tick taught, copy units.
Other staffView.
Students & parentsSee the plan; open lessons once they're shared.

Steps

  1. Open a class → Start planning (or Open the curriculum).
  2. Click New unit: Unit title, Starts, Ends, Summary (optional) → Add unit.
  3. In the unit, open Add a lesson: Lesson title, Date (defaults to the next timetabled lesson), Objective (optional), and tick the Standards this lesson teaches → Add lesson.
  4. After teaching, click Mark taught: a red tick appears in the margin. Undo clears it.
  5. Watch Standards coverage: "2 of 6 taught" with a chip per standard: Taught Planned Not planned.

Copy units forward

/classes/{class}/plan → Copy units from another class

Reuse units and lessons from another class (or last year's class). Copied lessons are re-dated to this class's timetable from the day you choose, skipping school holidays.

  1. On the class's curriculum page, find Copy units from another class.
  2. Choose From (classes with the same subject are listed first).
  3. Under Units, untick any you don't want (all are ticked).
  4. Set First lesson on and click Copy units.
  5. You'll see "Copied 2 units and 8 lessons, planned 15 Nov to 10 Dec."
  • Without a timetable, lessons keep their original spacing.
  • Up to 40 units per copy.

Teach & assess

Lecture Studio: AI narrated lectures

/classes/{class}/lectures

Upload slides and the Lecture Studio writes a narration for each slide, records a narrated video, writes a transcript, and drafts a quiz. You review the narration before anything is recorded, and publish when it's right.

Teacher / leadersMake, edit, publish and delete lectures.
Other staffView everything, no editing.
Students & parentsWatch published lectures with the transcript.

Make a lecture

  1. On the class page, click Make a lecture → New lecture.
  2. Give a Title, upload Slides (PDF, PowerPoint .pptx, or notes as text/Markdown, up to 50 MB), choose the Narration language (same as the slides, English or Arabic), and adjust the Instructions for the narrator.
  3. Click Make the narration. You can leave the page; it updates itself.
  4. When it says Narration ready for review, read each Slide box, edit, and Save narration.
  5. Click Make the video. When it's Ready, watch it with its transcript.
  6. Click Publish to 7A Mathematics. Optional: Make an online quiz turns the AI quiz into a real online quiz.

Question bank & AI Quiz Studio

/questions

A shared bank of multiple-choice questions tagged with standards. Write questions yourself, or paste a lesson and let the AI Quiz Studio draft them. Drafts usually appear within a minute, and nothing joins the bank until you approve it.

Write a question

  1. Under New question, type the Question and up to six Answers; pick the right one with its radio button.
  2. Add Why it's right (shown after marking) and tick Standards it checks → Add to the bank.

Draft questions with AI

  1. Fill in What are they for? (e.g. "Unit rates check").
  2. Paste notes or a text into Material (up to 20,000 characters), or leave it empty to write from the standards you tick.
  3. Choose How many (3, 5, 8, 10 or 15), the Language, optional Instructions, and Standards to tag them with.
  4. Click Draft questions. A panel AI drafts · Unit rates check shows "Writing…", then "5 to review".
  5. Untick any you don't want and click Add the ticked questions. They're labelled "AI-written, approved by [you]".

Rubrics, comment bank, weights & extensions

on the marking page

Mark against named criteria and the total adds itself up. Keep the comments you use often one click away. Give a student more time without changing everyone's due date.

Add a rubric

  1. On the marking page, open Add a rubric.
  2. For each criterion, type a name (e.g. "Uses the right units"), optionally What earns full points, and its points. Use Add a criterion for more.
  3. Click Save rubric: "Rubric saved. The work is now out of 20."
  4. Each student now gets one box per criterion and a running Total.

Comment bank

  1. In Your saved comments, type a comment (up to 300 characters) and click Save.
  2. Under any student's feedback box, click the comment to add it. Your comments follow you to every marking sheet.

Extension

  1. On the student's row, set Extension until. Their due date shows as "due 12 Nov (extended)" and a hand-in before then isn't late.

Online quizzes

…/assessments/{work} · …/take

Multiple-choice quizzes from the question bank, answered in Edulas and marked automatically.

Teacher

  1. Create an assessment with Students answer it online ticked.
  2. In Add from the question bank, click Add next to each question (questions with the quiz's standards come first).
  3. Click Open to students. Later, Close the quiz (and Reopen if needed).
  4. Check Handed in ("4 of 5", See answers) and Return to students so they see their score and answers.

Student

  1. On Today's To do, click Take the quiz.
  2. Choose one answer per question and click Hand in. You can hand in once.
  3. After it's returned, see your score and each answer marked right or not right, with the explanation.
Once any student has answered, the quiz's questions are locked.

Handing in work

…/assessments/{work}/hand-in

Students hand in assignments: write in the box, attach a file, or both. Teachers see it on the marking sheet; parents can view it.

  1. Click Hand in from Today's To do or the class's assessments.
  2. Read How it's marked if there's a rubric.
  3. Type in Your work and/or attach a File (a document, a photo of your work, slides or audio, up to 20 MB).
  4. Click Hand in: "Handed in."

A teacher can change things only in the classes they teach. School staff added to a class as a teacher get the teacher's powers for that class.

Words & rules

Secure

80% or more on the work that assesses a standard.

Developing

60% to 79%.

Not yet

Under 60%. Not a fail: not there yet.

Taught, not learned

Taught and assessed, and the class average is under 60% or half the class is Not yet.

Taught, not assessed

Lessons ticked taught, but no marked work on it yet.

Returned

Students and parents see marks, feedback and rubric scores only after the teacher returns the work.

Term average

Average of returned work due in the term; missing work left out. Weighted by Assignments / Quizzes / Tests if set.

Attended

Present or late, out of lessons recorded. Excused absences are left out.

Early warning

Attendance under 90%, a class average under 60%, 2+ standards Not yet in a class, or 2+ missing pieces this term.

Shared

A lesson page students and their parents can open.

Published

A lecture the class can watch, or a term whose report cards students and parents can see.

Extension

A later due date for one student; their hand-in isn't late before it.

Limits & things you can't undo

Sizes and counts
  • Lesson files and hand-ins: up to 20 MB.
  • Lecture slides: up to 50 MB (PDF, .pptx, text or Markdown; save old .ppt as .pptx first).
  • Spreadsheet import: 500 rows at a time. Standards: 300 per paste.
  • Invite links: one use, one email address, 14 days.
  • Absence reports: up to 60 days. Calendar items: up to a year.
  • Rubrics: up to 20 criteria. Questions: 2 to 6 answers.
These ask first, then can't be undone
  • Deleting a unit and its lessons.
  • Deleting a piece of work and all its marks.
  • Deleting a lecture, its video and its quiz.
  • Deleting a form and every answer.
  • Removing a term (its report comments go too).
  • Removing someone from the school.
  • Online quiz questions lock once a student answers; a student hands in a quiz once.
Not available yet: invitation emails sent by Edulas, a "Forgot password" link, and adding campuses after setup. For now the administrator shares invite links, and campuses are set when the school is created.
